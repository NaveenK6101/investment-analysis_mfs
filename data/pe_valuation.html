P/E Valuation

Where each index's price-to-earnings sits against its own history, why it moved (price or earnings), and what the next 1, 3 or 5 years looked like from similar valuations in the past.

Valuation describes the starting point; it is not a timing signal. Cheap entries have paid well over three years, but expensive readings are less reliable: in 2020 the Nifty P/E reached the high 30s because earnings fell, not because prices were euphoric, and the three years that followed returned well. How to read this
  • P/E is NSE's own trailing figure for each index, computed on standalone earnings. Consolidated-earnings P/E differs. Implied EPS is price divided by that P/E, so it follows NSE's constituent changes and trails the market by a quarter or so.
  • Windows over a year are shown as compound yearly rates (CAGR), with the cumulative total beside them where space allows.
  • Returns are total return (dividends included) where the total-return index exists, price return before that; annualised. Past returns are not forecasts, and costs and tax are not included.
  • History is short for some indices: Midcap 150 and Smallcap 250 only have P/E from 2016, Auto and Metal from 2012. The last few years have no forward outcome yet. A band with few episodes may reflect one market cycle.
  • Not meaningful: when an index's earnings are close to zero its P/E explodes (Midcap 150, Smallcap 250, Auto and Realty all show readings in the hundreds or more around 2016–2020). Months with a P/E above 100 are left out of every average, percentile, chart and band, and the count is shown above.
  • Zones are percentiles of the index's own history: below 20th Cheap, 20–40 Below average, 40–60 Fair, 60–80 Above average, 80–95 Expensive, 95+ Very expensive.
